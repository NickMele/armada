# What is a guide, and what does its figure show?

**Decided 2026-09-25.**

Fourteen guides shipped as paragraphs. He pinned: *"I would prefer guides be
animations or steps, not just a wall of text. Why can you agents not learn this.
We need to come up with a strategy for how guides are written and presented."*
Three shapes were built for him to compare — prose, steps, and figure-first.

**Chosen:** Both. A figure over numbered steps, in one guide.

**And the figure is the real app moving.** *"I assumed the figure was an
animation of the real app."* Not a diagram about the app — the app's own
components, animating. A game tutorial shows you the game.

**Cost he took:** A figure built from real components is bound to those
components, so a screen that changes can break its own guide. That is the
opposite of a diagram, which drifts silently instead. He took the breakage over
the drift.

**The second finding, in his words:** *"'What completes a job', but nothing says
'What is a job' and 'How to dispatch a job' and 'How to customize a job'."*

The cause: every guide in the catalogue exists because a sentence was deleted
off a screen in the #1602 sweep. Each is a footnote to what used to be on a
board. Nothing was ever written forward, from what a person needs to know — so
the catalogue answers narrow questions well and the first question not at all.

**Where it landed:** #1613 was the comparison, not the build. Annotation
`20260925-143538-x8iv`.

**What it says about him:** he judged a catalogue by whether it opens at the
beginning, not by whether each entry is good. See
[[2026-09-23-facts-on-screen-guides-behind-a-mark]] for the rule that put the
prose here in the first place — it said where an explanation goes, and never
what the set of them should cover.
